Data Collection Guide · 01 — Overview

Your advocate is only as good as its evidence.

Think of everything you would carry into the interview of your life — and everything a background check would ever ask for. You gather it once, thoroughly. Your advocate carries it forever, verified, so employers never have to ask twice.

§1What happens to what you collect

Every recording, document, and link you provide is broken down into individual facts — “led the payments migration in 2021”, “B.E. in Computer Science, 2015”, “mentored four juniors”. Each fact is then cross-checked against every other piece of evidence you provided: a manager’s words confirming your story, a certificate anchoring a claim, a public profile matching a date. Facts that hold up become your advocate’s voice; how well they hold up becomes your Authenticity Score.

The one principle

Nothing is taken on your word alone. That is the entire point — it’s why an employer can trust what your advocate says. So collect evidence the way a rigorous verifier would want it: independent people, hard documents, precise dates. This guide shows you exactly how.

§2The evidence pack — what to collect

A complete pack has five families of evidence. They are not interchangeable: each one does a job the others can’t, and the strongest profiles draw on all five.

Documents & records strongest weight

20–30 items

Degrees, marksheets, certificates, offer & experience letters, exam scorecards, award citations, signed recommendation letters. These are your anchors — the hardest evidence there is. Full checklist →

People speaking for you strong when specific

4–6 recorded calls

Managers, mentors or professors, peers, clients — calls of up to 20 minutes, recorded with consent; book a second session rather than running long. Add one expert interview per major career theme. Scripts & questions →

Captured moments supporting

1–3 recordings

A real product demo, a project pitch to stakeholders, a conference talk, an award ceremony, a performance. Moments where your work happened in front of witnesses.

Public profiles verifiable links

all that apply

LinkedIn, GitHub, published apps, scholarly profiles, portfolio sites. Anyone can check these — which is exactly why they count.

Your own words context, not proof

2–3 hrs + resume

Your self-interview, resume, and 3–5 written accomplishment stories. This is the spine of your advocate’s voice — but on its own it proves little. Everything you say here should be backed by something above.

Why five families?

Because verification is triangulation. A story you tell, retold independently by a manager, anchored by a document, visible in a public profile — that’s a fact nobody can argue with. One source alone, however good, is just one source.

§3How evidence is weighed

Different evidence carries different weight on its own, before any cross-checking. Knowing the ladder tells you where your collection effort pays off most.

Weight classWhat falls hereWhy
Anchor Degrees, certificates, licenses, employment letters, exam scorecards, patents, press coverage, competition results, GitHub history, published apps — and testimony from a recognized expert. Issued or witnessed by an institution or authority with nothing to gain. Very hard to fake, easy to verify.
Supporting Recorded words of managers, mentors, professors, peers, and clients; recordings of real moments (demos, talks, ceremonies); LinkedIn and social profiles. Real people staking their name on you, or real moments on tape — strong, but human memory and goodwill need corroboration.
Context Everything authored by you: self-interview, resume, stories, blog posts, portfolio — and words from family or unidentified sources. Invaluable for voice and detail, but self-reported. It becomes strong when an anchor or a supporting voice confirms it.
Common mistake

Collecting 40 items from one family — usually self-written material — and calling it done. Ten anchors plus four independent voices will beat a hundred pages of your own words, every time. Breadth across families beats volume within one.

§4Five golden rules

  1. Consent first, on tape. Everyone you record must agree — out loud, at the start of the recording — to be recorded for this purpose. Understand that what you upload permanently becomes part of your advocate’s evidence, so collect deliberately.
  2. Truth over polish. The verification process structurally rewards honesty: a well-evidenced unflattering fact strengthens your profile, while an inflated claim that nothing backs up weakens it. Bring your failures and weaknesses — with context.
  3. Specifics over adjectives. “Improved checkout conversion 12% in Q3 2022” can be verified; “results-driven team player” cannot. Names, dates, numbers — in every recording and every document.
  4. Independent voices, never scripted. Tell your endorsers which projects to talk about, never what to say. Two people describing the same event in their own words is powerful corroboration; two people reciting the same lines is worthless.
  5. One story, everywhere. Your resume, your interviews, your documents, and your profiles must agree — especially on dates and titles. Inconsistencies get flagged and dragged out into the open; get them straight before you start.

§5A realistic collection plan

Most people complete a strong pack in two to three weeks of part-time effort:

WeekFocusOutput
Week 1 Documents sweep + resume cleanup. Dig out every certificate, letter, and scorecard; fix your resume against the resume guidelines; line up your endorsers and book the calls. 20–30 documents scanned · resume final · 4–6 calls scheduled
Week 2 Record. Endorser calls (up to 20 min each — schedule follow-ups rather than running long), your self-interview in short takes, and one expert interview per major theme, using the scripts and question banks. Convert audio to FLAC as you go. ~6 hours of recordings, as FLAC
Week 3 Round out. Demo or pitch recording, public profile links, written stories. Then a final pass with the score levers: any major claim still resting on your word alone? Get it a second source. Complete pack, cross-checked by you before we cross-check it

Gather the pack completely before submitting — verification works on the whole picture, and a complete pack scores meaningfully better than a trickle of fragments.
