Sales Rep Guide · 04 — Quality & Boundaries

Make it accurate, and honest about its limits.

A great folder isn't just big — it's fresh, official, complete where it counts, and clear about what the rep must never say. This page has three parts: the four levers that decide quality, a self-test for coverage, and the boundary declarations that become the rep's refusal training.

§1The four quality levers

Everything you do to the folder moves one of these four. They're worth knowing by name.

1 · Freshness

How current the material is. Every doc carries a last-updated date; the system detects and flags stale material rather than trusting it blindly. A dated pile you refresh beats a bigger pile you forget about.

2 · Officialness

Whether an answer comes from something published or something rough. Published beats draft — so mark which is which. Label finished, approved material as official; flag anything provisional as draft so the rep leans on the solid stuff first.

3 · Coverage

How much of what customers actually ask, the folder can answer. This is the lever that decides how often the rep helps versus hands off — and it's the one most worth measuring, which is what the self-test below is for.

4 · Boundaries

How clearly the rep knows what it must not do — the claims it can't make, the limits it must state exactly, the moment to fetch a human. Good boundaries are what let you trust an always-on rep with your customers.

§2The coverage self-test

You can't feel coverage by looking at a folder — you have to test it against real questions. Here's the worksheet. It takes an afternoon and it will find your gaps before your customers do.

  1. Write the 30 questions customers actually ask you. Not the ones you wish they asked — the real ones. Pull them from your inbox, your chat logs, your sales calls. Get to 30; the boring and awkward ones matter most.
  2. For each question, find the answer in your pile. Point to the exact document, page, or recording that answers it. If you can put your finger on it, that question is covered.
  3. Mark every question you can't answer from the pile. Those are your gaps — each one a customer question the rep will either escalate or, worse, guess at.
  4. Fill the gaps or declare them. Either add material that answers the question, or add it to the boundaries below so the rep hands off cleanly instead of improvising.

§3The boundary declarations

This is the most important worksheet in the guide. Everything above teaches the rep what to say; this teaches it what to refuse. Your answers here become the rep's exam and its refusal training — the rep is drilled and tested on holding these lines, so be exact.

a · The never-say list

Write your real answer to each. "We don't have a fixed rule" is itself an answer — it means the rep escalates.

AreaDeclare
Refund limits The exact refund policy the rep may state — window, conditions, what's non-refundable. Anything outside it, the rep must not promise; it hands off.
Legal / medical / financial claims The rep makes no legal, medical, or financial advice or guarantees, ever. List any regulated claims specific to your field that it must avoid.
Competitor policy What the rep may say about rivals — typically: stay factual about your own product, never disparage, never speculate about a competitor's internals or pricing.
Discount authority Whether the rep can offer discounts at all, and if so the exact ceiling. If it can't: it never invents a deal — it routes discount requests to a person.

b · The escalation route

Every boundary needs a landing spot. Declare where the human handoff points are, so "I'll get a person" actually reaches one.

  • The default handoff: where does a customer go when the rep can't or won't answer — a support email, a live-chat queue, a booking link, a phone number?
  • Billing & refunds beyond the stated policy — who handles it, and how does the rep route there?
  • Sales & discounts beyond the rep's authority — which person or team, and what does the rep say while handing off?
  • Anything urgent or sensitive — outages, security concerns, an upset customer — the fast path to a human, stated plainly.
  • Hours & expectations — if a handoff isn't instant, what should the rep tell the customer to expect (e.g. "someone will reply within one business day")?
These become the rep's exam

Treat this worksheet as binding. The declarations you write here are what the rep is tested against before it ever talks to a customer — a rep that crosses a boundary in testing gets retrained, not shipped. Vague boundaries make a rep that guesses; exact ones make a rep you can trust to say "I'll get a person" and mean it.
