Sales Rep Guide · 01 — Overview

Your rep only knows what you give it.

You are about to build an AI sales rep for your product. It will answer customers day and night — but only from the material you collect here. This page is the contract: what the rep can do, what it can't, and the one rule every file has to pass.

§1What your rep is — and isn't

Think of the rep as a brand-new hire who has read everything in one folder and nothing else. It has no memory of your business beyond what you supply, and — this is the part people forget — it cannot browse the web. It can't look up your latest blog post, can't check a competitor, can't find the answer it's missing. If a fact isn't in the folder, the rep does not have it.

So the folder decides everything. When a customer asks something the folder answers, the rep answers well. When they ask something it doesn't cover, you get one of two outcomes:

  • The good outcome: the rep says "I'll get a person for that" and hands off. A miss, but a safe one — the customer is routed, not misled.
  • The bad outcome: the rep guesses, and a confident wrong answer goes out under your name. That is the failure this guide exists to prevent.

The difference between those two outcomes is almost entirely coverage — how much of what customers actually ask is sitting in the folder, and how clearly the rep has been told where its edges are. Both are things you control here.

The one principle

A gap in the folder is a gap in the rep. You are not writing the rep's personality — you are handing it its knowledge and its limits. Collect the way you'd brief a sharp new hire on their first day: the real material, current, and an honest map of what they're not allowed to answer.

§2One rule for everything you supply: public or publishable

Whatever a customer could hear from the rep, the rep might say. So the test for every single file you add is simple: would you be comfortable if this appeared, word for word, on your public website? If yes, it belongs in the folder. If it makes you hesitate, it stays out.

  • Public — already out in the world: your site, your docs, your published pricing, your help center, your marketing.
  • Publishable — not published yet, but you'd be fine if it were: an internal FAQ you could post tomorrow, a demo script, a founder interview recorded for this purpose.
Nothing confidential — v1

Keep internal-only material out entirely: unreleased roadmaps, real customer data, contracts, internal cost or margin figures, credentials, anything under NDA. The rep can't keep a secret it's been handed — if it's in the folder, treat it as something a customer might repeat back to you. This first version of your rep is public knowledge only.
