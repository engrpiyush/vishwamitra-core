// 01 · the problem

Hiring runs on documents built to hide the truth.

The résumé is a marketing brochure. The interview is theatre. References go unanswered. The most important decisions about people rest on the least honest signals about them.

The paper lies politely

Résumés are written to inflate strengths and bury weaknesses. Everyone knows it. Everyone does it anyway — because nothing better has existed.

Keywords reject talent

Most large employers filter on keywords before a human reads a line. A brilliant candidate with the wrong phrasing never reaches the door.

Potential stays invisible

For early-career people the signal is weakest where it matters most. Too many to interview, too little on paper — the best are simply missed.

// 02 · the solution

Not a profile. Not a clone. An advocate.

Vishwakarma.ai builds a dedicated AI for each person — one that knows them deeply and speaks about them, the way a trusted colleague vouches for you in a room you're not in. Honest about strengths. Honest about gaps. Always theirs.

// 03 · the method

Four steps from a person to their advocate.

A reviewed, auditable pipeline — from raw signal to a deployed, conversational AI an employer can simply talk to.

01_LISTEN

Listen

A structured conversation with the individual, plus their real professional footprint — history, projects, contributions.

02_ENDORSE

Endorse

Expert-facilitated, consent-based interviews with the people who've actually worked beside them. Stories, not statements.

03_TRAIN

Train

Everything is curated into reviewed training data and tuned into a dedicated model on managed, enterprise-grade infrastructure.

04_DEPLOY

Converse

The advocate goes live at a shareable link. Employers ask anything; every interaction is logged and auditable.

// 06 · faq

The things investors ask first.

No — and this is the heart of the design. The model never impersonates anyone. It is a distinct advocate that speaks about a person, the way a trusted colleague would vouch for you. It's candid about both strengths and weaknesses, because credibility depends on honesty, not flattery.

The endorser layer. The interface and the model can be copied; the network of real, consent-verified testimony from people who've actually worked with someone cannot be acquired quickly. It requires genuine relationships, real conversations, and a trusted process — which compounds over time into a structural moat.

Both sides of the marketplace. Individuals pay to build, host, and update their advocate across their career. Employers pay for access — per conversation, by subscription, or via enterprise API. As the platform scales, employer revenue becomes the primary driver while individual fees cover delivery cost.

Consent is designed in from the first step. Every endorser participates knowingly, every data source is authorised by the individual, and every employer interaction is logged and auditable. The person owns and controls what their advocate is built from.

Active proof-of-concept, with the core data-labelling and managed fine-tuning pipeline already built and running. The first advocate is in development now, with a launch targeted for 2026. We're in early conversations with investors, incubators, and accelerators.

Capable, efficiently-tuneable models and managed training infrastructure only became practical very recently. Building a dedicated, high-quality AI per person was prohibitively expensive two years ago. The cost curve and the tooling have finally caught up to the idea.
